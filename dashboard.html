A Download button in the popup on a Vimeo video saves it as one .mp4, picture and sound together, at the quality the player is on. It is always there — on Vimeo it is the only thing Bell Bearer adds, and it does nothing until pressed. Where a download is saved, a folder inside Downloads or asked every time, is set with YouTube's, under YouTube.

A watch party plays an episode in sync for everyone in it, each on their own Netflix account. Start or join one from the popup on the episode. Your name in the room and how a party connects are set in Settings, under Watch party.

How long you mean to spend on x.com in a day is set in the one list that covers every site — Settings → General, "Hold me to a daily limit on". Time counts only while a tab of the site is on screen and this window has focus. The tally is today's and nothing older than today is stored — at midnight it is gone, not archived.

Promoted posts are found by the tracking wrapper Twitter puts around a paid placement, not by the word "Promoted", so this works whatever language the site is in. Nothing is deleted — hidden entries keep their place in the site's own bookkeeping, which is what stops the timeline jumping as you scroll.

What Bell Bearer adds to Twitter's own rows, each off until you ask for it. The pouch itself is always there — the Pouch row in Twitter's left navigation, the panel it opens, and the People and Posts tabs here.

The bookmark is hidden rather than deleted, so nothing you have already bookmarked on Twitter is touched and turning this back off puts the button where it was. The pouch button stands in the bookmark's place when both are on.

How long you mean to spend on reddit.com in a day is set in the one list that covers every site — Settings → General, "Hold me to a daily limit on". Time counts only while a tab of the site is on screen and this window has focus. The tally is today's and nothing older than today is stored — at midnight it is gone, not archived.

Your LeetCode username, used to draw the practice heat map from LeetCode's own record rather than only from what Bell Bearer watched — which starts the day you installed it. The request is public and carries no cookies: it tells LeetCode nothing about who is asking, and works signed out. Leave it empty to keep the map local.

Bell Bearer times each problem with LeetCode's own stopwatch rather than a clock of its own, so the number on the page and the number recorded against the attempt are the same number. Whether a submission passed is read from LeetCode's own network calls, not from the result banner — and pressing Run is not a submission, so it never counts against you.

Scheduling is FSRS-5, the algorithm Anki's newer scheduler uses, with its published weights. Target retention is the chance of remembering a problem at the moment it comes back around: raise it and problems return sooner and more often, lower it and you see each one less but forget more of them. Ninety percent is the value the algorithm is tuned for; below seventy or above ninety-seven it stops behaving sensibly, so the field will not go there.

Mock interview mode hides what an interview would not give you — the difficulty, the topic tags, the hints, the similar questions and the solutions tab — and runs a countdown. Nothing is deleted; leaving the mode puts the page back exactly as it was. Ctrl+Alt+M starts one on the problem you are reading, whether or not this is on.

Code templates are written by you — nothing ships with the extension. Select working code in LeetCode's editor and press Ctrl+Alt+T to save it as one, and the same keys offer them back, filtered to the language the editor is in. That shortcut has its own key handling so that it works with the caret inside the editor, where every other shortcut deliberately stays out of the way.

Bell Bearer measures a race from the keystrokes themselves rather than from TypeRacer's scoreboard — which is what makes the weak-spot tables possible at all, since the site publishes none of it. Nothing is sent anywhere and no raw keystrokes are kept: each race is folded into a table of letter pairs at the end and the stream is thrown away. Speed and accuracy are read off the result panel as a cross-check only.

The typing caret is the marker showing where you are in the quote, not the mouse pointer. Leaving it on TypeRacer's own draws nothing and changes nothing on the page. Any other shape hides the site's own marking while ours is up and puts it straight back the moment you switch away — and if the quote's markup cannot be read, no caret is drawn rather than one in the wrong place. Ctrl+Alt+C cycles the shapes from the page itself.

The sounds are synthesised rather than played from a file, so there is nothing to load before the first keystroke can click and no audio ships with the extension. The volume is a percent and above 100 it really is louder — Web Audio gain is not capped the way an audio element's volume is. At 0 nothing is played at all rather than played silently.

Preferred Subtitles

Shorts has no way to skip within a short — ↑ and ↓ change short, and nothing seeks. With this on, ← and → skip back and forward while a Shorts page is open; everywhere else the arrow keys are left alone. Both keys can be rebound under Shortcuts.

A Download button in the popup on a video saves it as one .mp4 — picture and sound together — at the quality the player is on. YouTube is asked for the streams the way its own apps ask, the way yt-dlp does; progress shows on the video's page, and the download carries on there if the popup closes.

The reader puts YouTube's own player beside the video's transcript, with your checkpoints under it — the same player, moved rather than copied, so it keeps playing, at the speed and with the captions it had. Open it from the popup or with Ctrl+Alt+R.
